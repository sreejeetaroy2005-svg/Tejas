# Tejas — Modeling Assumptions

All data in this prototype is **synthetic demonstration data**, generated by
`backend/scripts/synthetic_data_generator.py`. It is not derived from, and
should never be presented as, real Oil India field data.

## Why synthetic data, and what it's for

The goal is to produce data with the *right qualitative relationships* so the
risk engine, optimizer, and dashboard can be built and demoed convincingly.
The exact numeric values are illustrative, not field-calibrated.

## CSS cycle structure

- One cycle = injection (2-4 days) → soak (3-6 days) → production (30-55 days),
  then repeats.
- Cycle lengths are randomized within these ranges per cycle to avoid an
  unrealistically perfect, repeating pattern.

## Temperature model

- Reservoir temperature rises toward a peak (~125°C) during injection,
  plateaus during soak, then decays exponentially back toward a cold
  baseline (~45°C) during production, with a decay time constant of ~10 days.
- This means temperature (and everything downstream of it) changes
  *fastest* early in the production stage and *slows down* as it approaches
  baseline — matching how heat loss to surrounding rock typically behaves.

## Viscosity model

- Modeled as an exponential function of temperature: viscosity is highest
  (~8000 cP) at baseline temperature and lowest (~150 cP) at peak
  temperature, calibrated so viscosity smoothly bridges the two.
- This is a simplification of real heavy-oil viscosity-temperature
  relationships (which are often fit from lab PVT data); it is directionally
  correct but not calibrated to Baghewala-specific crude properties.

## Production model

- Oil rate is modeled as a function of (a) how easily oil flows at current
  viscosity, and (b) natural decline over the course of the production
  stage (reservoir energy depleting), independent of temperature.
- Water cut and gas rate are simple proportional approximations, not
  reservoir-simulated.

## Pump (SRP) behavior — the core of the risk story

- The operator's pump setpoint (SPM ~9, VFD ~42 Hz) does **not**
  automatically adjust as viscosity rises. This is intentional: it
  represents the operational gap the risk engine and recommendation
  engine are meant to catch and correct.
- **Fillage** (how much of each pump stroke is actually filled with fluid)
  drops as viscosity rises and as SPM runs ahead of what the well can
  supply. Low fillage is the leading indicator of rod-floating / fluid-pound
  risk.
- **Motor current** and **rod load** rise with viscosity and spike further
  when fillage is low, approximating the mechanical shock loading that
  occurs when a rod pump strokes without enough fluid underneath it.

## Rod-floating risk score (0-100)

A transparent, rule-based weighted score, not a black-box model:

```
risk = 0.35 * fillage_component
     + 0.30 * viscosity_component
     + 0.20 * spm_excess_component
     + 0.15 * motor_current_component
```

- `Low` ≤ 35, `Medium` 36-65, `High` > 65.
- These weights and thresholds are **demo assumptions**, kept in code as
  named constants so they can be tuned later with domain-expert input —
  they are not derived from failure data.

## What this data is good enough for

- Demonstrating a believable rise-and-fall cycle for temperature → viscosity
  → fillage → risk, which is the core narrative of the Tejas demo.
- Exercising the risk engine, optimizer, and dashboard end-to-end.

## What this data is NOT good enough for

- Any claim about actual Baghewala field performance, savings, emissions,
  or accuracy.
- Training a model intended for real deployment without field validation.
